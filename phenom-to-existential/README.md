# Embodiment and the Roots of HCI

**Parallel Developments in Philosophy and Computing**

Jo Cichon · Northeastern University, Seattle
Presented at the ACM Celebration of Cascadia Women in Computing (CWIC 2025), October 24, 2025

---

## Overview

This project uses Paul Dourish's account of embodied interaction in *Where the Action Is* (2001) as a starting point. From there it traces a parallel between the history of philosophy and the history of computing.

Early computing inherited Cartesian dualism: interaction was symbolic, procedural, and abstract. Meanwhile, Husserl, Heidegger, Merleau-Ponty, and Wittgenstein were already challenging the split between mind and body. Decades later, HCI made a similar turn. It moved through graphical interfaces to tangible and social computing, and toward context, situatedness, and lived experience.

The project closes by proposing existentialism as the next step. Phenomenology grounds perception in lived experience. Existentialism adds responsibility, choice, and ethical agency, which bear directly on how AI systems are designed today.

## Key insights

- **Asymmetrical parallel.** Philosophy critiqued abstraction decades, or centuries, before similar turns appeared in technology.
- **Structured sequence.** Philosophy moved from dualism to phenomenology, embodiment, and existentialism. Computing followed a similar path: symbolic computing, then GUIs, then tangible and social computing, then AI ethics.
- **Shared tension.** In both fields, the pull between abstraction and embodiment is not a problem to solve. It is a dialectic that drives change.

### Paired figures

| Philosophy                                  | Computing                                                    |
|---------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Descartes: mind–body dualism                | Turing / von Neumann: abstract computation                   |
| Husserl: intentionality                     | C.A.R. Hoare: Communicating Sequential Processes (CSP)       |
| Heidegger: being-in-the-world               | Robin Milner: CCS and the π-calculus                         |
| Merleau-Ponty: the lived body               | Jonathan Grudin: "the computer reaches out"                  |
| Wittgenstein: meaning through use           | Paul Dourish: embodied interaction                           |
| Existentialists: agency, freedom, responsibility | Linda Stein: programmers are in the world too           |

## Relationship to Mirror or Self-Portrait

This project came before [Mirror or Self-Portrait](../). It ends by pointing to existentialism as a foundation for thinking about AI. The later project takes up that thread and uses existentialism as the conceptual anchor of its two-axis framework.
